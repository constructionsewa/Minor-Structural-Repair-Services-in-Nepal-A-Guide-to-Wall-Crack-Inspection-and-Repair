## What Are Minor Structural Repairs?

Minor structural repairs involve addressing localised building damage that does not necessarily indicate major foundation or load-bearing failure.

Common examples include:

- Hairline cracks in walls and plaster
- Cracks around doors and windows
- Minor settlement-related cracks
- Localised plaster damage
- Repairs following plumbing leaks or moisture infiltration
- Small masonry repairs and crack stitching

## Common Causes of Wall Cracks in Nepal

Several factors can contribute to cracks in Nepali residential buildings:

1. **Seismic movement:** Earthquakes and tremors can cause cracks around openings and wall corners.
2. **Foundation settlement:** Uneven ground settlement can produce diagonal or stepped cracks in masonry.
3. **Moisture infiltration:** Plumbing leaks, poor drainage, and roof leakage can weaken plaster and masonry.
4. **Temperature changes:** Expansion and contraction of building materials can contribute to cracking.
5. **Poor construction practices:** Inadequate curing, weak mortar mixtures, and insufficient reinforcement can contribute to cracks.
6. **Age and general wear:** Older buildings may develop cracks that require inspection.

## Common Wall Crack Repair Methods

The appropriate repair method depends on the crack's location, cause, depth, and movement.

- Flexible fillers for suitable fine cracks
- Polymer-modified mortar for selected masonry and plaster repairs
- Epoxy or polyurethane injection for appropriate deeper cracks
- Crack stitching with reinforcement for suitable wider masonry cracks
- Replastering and repainting after the underlying problem has been addressed

## When Should You Seek Professional Inspection?

Consider a professional assessment if:

- A crack continues to widen over time.
- Diagonal or stepped cracks extend through brickwork.
- Nearby doors or windows become difficult to close.
- A wall develops visible bulging or tilting.
- Cracks repeatedly return after previous repairs.

Suspected structural damage should be assessed by a qualified structural engineer rather than treated as a simple cosmetic repair.
